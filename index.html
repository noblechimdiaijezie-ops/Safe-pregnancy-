Here is the thing most women are never told

The most important weeks happen before you even know you're pregnant.

In the first weeks of pregnancy, your baby's brain, spine and heart begin to form. That often happens before a woman knows she is pregnant.

That is why some of the most useful steps — like folic acid, reviewing your medicines, checking vaccines and understanding your own cycle — work best when you start early.

But instead of clear guidance, most women get a mess of conflicting posts, videos, WhatsApp forwards and "advice" from everyone.

You end up confused, overwhelmed, or not sure what to ask when you finally meet a healthcare professional.

This book fixes that. It puts everything in one calm, sensible order, so you know what to do, what to ask, and when to get help.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is this medical advice?

No. It is a general educational guide. It does not replace an individual medical assessment, and it cannot diagnose or treat any condition. It tells you clearly when to speak to a doctor, nurse, midwife or pharmacist.

Will this guarantee that I get pregnant?

No honest book can. It helps you prepare, understand your cycle and know when to ask for help, so you make informed choices instead of guessing.

I am not trying yet. Is it still useful?

Yes. Preparation usually takes weeks to a few months, so starting before you try gives you time to check your health, review medicines and vaccines, and build habits at your own pace.

My periods are irregular. Will this help?

Yes. There is a full chapter on irregular periods, and the whole book is written to cover both regular and irregular cycles.

Is it relevant in Nigeria?

Yes. It is written for readers in Nigeria, elsewhere in Africa and around the world. Where advice differs between countries, the book says so, and it focuses on steps that cost little or nothing.

Do I need a partner to use it?

No. You may have a partner or be planning on your own. The partner sections are optional.
